# Helsinki Live

Day 24 · Network · 30 Day Map Challenge 2026

Explore Helsinki Regional Transport buses on Tileflow Streets. The map opens north-up on the Helsinki metropolitan network with no vehicle selected. The overview fits the screen, from Espoo to eastern Helsinki and north to the airport. Click a bus to highlight its complete trip and stops in the public line’s colour while keeping the current camera view. At street zoom the selected trip follows the centre of the carriageway in its direction of travel. Sections without a reliable Streets match use the published HSL geometry. On desktop the trip card opens beside the vehicle and stays in place for reading. A short connector identifies the selected bus. On phones, the bottom sheet shows the next stop and arrival time. Tap **More stops** to see the remaining stops. The card displays the selected line number while the other buses retain their roof badges. Use the bus picker beside the map title to choose any active bus by line, destination and vehicle number. It centres the vehicle at the current zoom. Choose **Bus network** to return to the regional overview. At street zoom, unselected buses show a short guide in the same punctuality colour as their body. The route lines provide a visual reference while you explore buses and stops. Select a stop to open its departures card. A connector points to the selected boarding point as you pan or rotate the map. Open a departure to keep its boarding estimate on the bus card, or open a stop from a bus and use **Back** to restore the previous view. The full trip uses one colour and width from end to end. Chevrons pulse in travel order along its streets. Selecting a bus enables gentle following at the current zoom and orientation. The camera leaves room for small movements. Dragging the map or using its camera controls pauses following. Use **Resume follow** to continue from the current view, or **Locate bus** to bring a hidden vehicle back into view. Reduced-motion preferences keep following off. Choose **Fit route** to zoom to the complete trip. Helsinki’s solar position selects the native Streets `timeOfDay` preset (dawn, day, dusk or night). Streets supplies the building materials and lighting. The controls follow the same period.

The overview colours each bus and its recent trail by the predicted arrival at its next served stop. At regional zoom the luminous trail shows up to one minute or 800 m of observed movement and fades as the signal ages. Green covers early arrivals and delays under two minutes, orange covers two to under five minutes late, and red covers five or more minutes late. Grey indicates a missing comparison. The percentage expected late counts the orange and red groups among matched predictions, across the entire HSL bus feed. Coverage includes every bus with a position less than 30 seconds old, regardless of the camera or selected route. The bus and stop cards give the expected time, scheduled time and difference when available. The **Next arrivals** percentage stays visible beneath the map title. On phones, tap that row to see the counts and legend. The adjacent **Map controls** button opens the camera tools. At street scale the body shows the next-stop prediction. Opening the trip switches the body to the public line colour used by its badge, route and stop rings. Closing it restores the current punctuality colour.

## Run

From the repository root, install with `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` and run `pnpm --filter @tileflow-demos/helsinki-live dev`. For a production build use the same filter with `build` and `preview`.

## Transit data

- [HSL GTFS-RT](https://hsldevcom.github.io/gtfs_rt/) supplies observed vehicle locations and trip updates. The browser polls positions every three seconds and predictions every fifteen seconds. The service date, start time, route and direction identify a trip. A vehicle moves only between observations. Its last fix ages out after 90 seconds. The stop board uses live predictions and links to HSL's timetable.
- [HSL high-frequency positioning](https://digitransit.fi/en/developers/apis/5-realtime-api/vehicle-positions/high-frequency-positioning/) supplies one-second position and door readings where available for the selected bus. The selected bus opens its doors when an HFP reading reports an opening. The feed provides a combined door state, so the two leaves of the model’s kerbside door move together.
- [HSL's published GTFS ZIP](https://infopalvelut.storage.hsldev.com/gtfs/hsl.zip) supplies the route shapes, stop order and stop names. The catalogue includes its publication date and validity range. Install Shapely 2 for catalogue preparation. Refresh with `python3 scripts/prepare_network.py /path/to/hsl.zip` before the catalogue expires. From the same GTFS ZIP, regenerate the scheduled arrival table with `python3 scripts/prepare_timetable.py /path/to/hsl.zip public/data/timetable.json`, then rebuild. Each feed publishes its service date range. Outside those dates the app reports punctuality as unknown until a new timetable covers the requested service day. Diversions can differ from the published street geometry.
- Transit data © Helsinki Regional Transport (HSL), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The initial catalogue was delivered on 24 November 2026. Map and map-data credits are provided by Streets.

## Stops on the street map

Select a bus to see its next platform. At street zoom the anchored card opens the stop’s departures. Two pause bars mean HSL reports the vehicle at that platform. The open-door symbol requires a recent door reading for the selected trip. The wheel hubs roll with distance along the trip.

From neighbourhood zoom (12.5), a yellow flag marks a street stop. A small roof above it identifies a confirmed shelter. Selecting a bus replaces the flags along its trip with route-coloured rings. The current stop is filled and named. Other stops use matching rings along the full trip. A physical stop model replaces its map sign in a close, tilted street view. Served stops keep a ring at their base.

From the city overview the interchange symbol and name group the platforms of an HSL terminal. At zoom 15.5 the terminal gives way to individual boarding points labelled **Platform 1**, **Platform 2**, and so on. Selecting the station lists all its served boarding points, including points identified only by their public stop code. Station membership comes from GTFS `parent_station`, `location_type` and the current bus catalogue, and the numbers from `platform_code`. [HSL’s central Helsinki interchange plan](https://www.hsl.fi/sites/default/files/uploads/helsinki_1730x1180_09102020.pdf) illustrates the separate boarding areas around the railway station. Regenerate these groups after refreshing the network with `python3 scripts/prepare_terminals.py /path/to/hsl.zip public/data/network.json public/data/terminals.json`.

Platform numbers and indoor levels come from HSL GTFS. At street scale, code-matched outdoor boarding points on the right of a directed trip show a stop flag with the real name and code. Surveyed amenities determine whether the model includes a bench or a glazed shelter. Existing mapped roof footprints take precedence. The furniture is a schematic interpretation of the [Helsinki street design guide](https://kaupunkitilaohje.hel.fi/kortti/pysakki/). Indoor or numbered platforms and stops with uncertain placement retain a map symbol. Winter-clad waiting passengers are illustrative. A selected bus with a fresh HSL door-open reading at the matching kerb triggers one illustrative exchange. Reduced-motion preferences keep the figures still.

The facilities extract is © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), ODbL 1.0. To refresh it after the GTFS catalogue, install Python’s `osmium` package and run `python3 scripts/prepare_facilities.py uusimaa-latest.osm.pbf` with the [Uusimaa extract](https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts/europe/finland/uusimaa-latest.osm.pbf).

## Roundabouts

The catalogue matches each trip’s incoming and outgoing streets to connected one-way roundabout geometry. Vehicle motion, bearings, forward previews and selected routes share the resulting arc. Stop offsets are remeasured along the corrected trip. Where a trip crosses the interior and the entry or exit is ambiguous, the route has a gap and the map can show the reported GPS location. Mapped flyovers and through roads are checked separately.

The directed circles and approach roads in `scripts/data/roundabouts.json` are © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), ODbL 1.0, sourced from the [Uusimaa extract](https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts/europe/finland/uusimaa-latest.osm.pbf). The derived road geometry is included in the published catalogue. To refresh the extract, install Python’s `osmium` and Shapely 2, run `python3 scripts/roundabouts.py uusimaa-latest.osm.pbf scripts/data/roundabouts.json`, then regenerate the GTFS catalogue.

## Vehicles and road placement

Every vehicle is drawn as Arco, a stylised city bus with a rounded roof, an inset windscreen and a two-leaf kerbside door. Its body shows punctuality until the rider selects the full trip. The vehicle then takes the colour of the line, which also appears on the route and stop rings. The bus has individual slate-blue windows and sliding doors. The roof badge carries the public line number and colour until the rider opens the bus’s trip card. Fleet IDs can identify the [Nobina U12](https://bussikirjasto.fi/esb/kortti.php?haku=YMN-923) in the vehicle card. Zoom in to inspect a bus at street level. The selected trip and its stop rings remain visible from the regional overview down to the street.

Positions are matched to the directed shape of the vehicle's trip using the reported next stop, ordered distances from the GTFS schedule, heading and recent progress. The model interpolates between observations along the trip. At junctions it follows a bounded curve between the incoming and outgoing lanes and averages directed street headings over the 12 m body, with a bounded correction from the street geometry. A shape that doubles back within that span can fall back to a location dot. Animation timing accounts for observed delivery intervals as well as the whole-second GPS timestamps. The guide follows the same lane as the 3D bus and fades to transparent approximately 12–28 m ahead of its front, depending on zoom. Selecting the bus replaces its guide with the full trip. At street zoom a served platform up to 550 m ahead can show its name and fresh arrival estimate independently of the guide’s length. At street zoom it also uses the drivable centreline from loaded Streets tiles, respecting one-way carriageways. At zoom 14.7 the regional dots give way to 3D buses. An unmatched vehicle keeps its GPS dot at street scale. This represents the vehicle's travel direction and an estimated road position; HSL does not publish a precise lane or individual door telemetry.

The dashboard credits Helsinki Region Transport with the [HSL/HRT mark from its developer site](https://github.com/HSLdevcom/dev.hsl.fi/blob/master/logo.png). The yellow street-stop pictogram echoes the flag on the 3D stop pole.

## Serving the demo

The repository's maps build mounts the demo at `/helsinki-live/`. Its two same-origin feed endpoints relay the public HSL protobuf responses, which lack browser CORS headers. Vite provides the endpoints in development and preview. The maps Worker provides them in the combined deployment. The WebSocket connection for the selected bus goes directly to HSL.
